# What's FIRE? editorial standards (#12.136)

Draft for Jonathan's approval. Nothing here changes the live site. It sets the rules every article follows, and the editorial policy page (see niche-rules.md, step 5) can be written from it.

## Sources
- Every figure, rate, limit or date in an article has a source named in the text or linked beside it. No figure without one.
- Prefer primary sources: IRS, Social Security Administration, Federal Reserve, BLS, Vanguard and other fund-company papers, and the original Trinity study and its updates. Use news and blogs only to point to those.
- State the period and the data set for any return or withdrawal figure ("historically, between 1926 and 2023, in U.S. stocks").
- No invented statistics, quotes, testimonials or personal stories. If a story is real, label it as one person's result.
- Every external link is opened and checked on the day the article ships. Affiliate links carry rel="sponsored".

## Fact-check steps before an article ships
1. List every claim that is a number, a rule, a limit or a date.
2. Match each to its source and read the source, not a summary of it.
3. Recalculate every worked example by hand or with the site's calculator.
4. Check tax and account limits against the current tax year's IRS figures.
5. Check that the article never promises an outcome ("you will", "guaranteed", "risk-free").
6. Confirm the Financial Disclaimer link sits near money guidance, and the Affiliate Disclosure near any affiliate link.
7. Record the check in the pull request: sources used, date checked, who checked.

## Updates
- Front matter carries `date` (first published) and `updated` (last substantive review). Change `updated` only when the facts were rechecked, never to look fresh.
- Articles with tax limits, rates or thresholds are rechecked each January and whenever the source changes.
- All other articles are reviewed at least every 6 months. The review reads the sources again, fixes what changed and notes it at the foot of the article.
- Corrections: fix the page, update the date, and add a one-line note saying what changed.

## Answer-first writing (for search and AI answers)
- The answer is in the first one or two sentences, in plain words, before any background.
- Headings are the questions people ask ("How much do I need for lean FIRE?"), one question per heading, with the answer directly under it.
- Define each term once, in a sentence that can stand alone if quoted.
- Use short lists and small tables for steps and comparisons. Put the date and the source next to each figure.
- Include a short FAQ only where the questions are real (from People also ask or Search Console), not to pad.
- Shape per brand-voice.md: question or plain title, one-sentence answer, explanation, one example, "what this does not mean", related articles, disclaimer line. About 800 to 1,500 words.

## Writing rules
- No em dashes. Use a comma, a period or a colon.
- Nothing that reads as AI-written: no stock phrases or filler openers, no three-item lists by habit, no summary paragraph that repeats the article. The banned and avoided word lists in brand-voice.md apply.
- Plain, warm, specific. A real example with real numbers beats "many people".
- Read the finished article aloud once. If a sentence sounds like nobody would say it, rewrite it.

## Authorship and review
- Each article has a byline and an author page that states the real basis for writing it (see the author decision in the brief).
- Educational only, not advice. No article tells a reader what to do with their own money.
