Your FIRE number is the single most important figure in any early retirement strategy. It's the portfolio size at which your investments generate enough passive income to cover your expenses — indefinitely.

Here's how to calculate it, the variations that matter, and what it looks like across different income levels.

## The Basic Formula

**FIRE Number = Annual Expenses ÷ Safe Withdrawal Rate**

Using the standard 4% rule:

**FIRE Number = Annual Expenses × 25**

That's it. If you spend $40,000 per year, your FIRE number is $1,000,000. If you spend $80,000, it's $2,000,000.

## Why the Number Is 25×

The "25× rule" comes from the **4% safe withdrawal rate** — established by the Trinity Study, a 1998 analysis of historical stock market returns. Researchers found that a portfolio of stocks and bonds could sustain a 4% annual withdrawal for 30 years without being depleted in 95% of historical scenarios.

Dividing your annual expenses by 4% (or 0.04) is mathematically equivalent to multiplying by 25.

$60,000 ÷ 0.04 = **$1,500,000**
$60,000 × 25 = **$1,500,000** ✓

## Step 1: Know Your Annual Expenses

Before you can calculate your FIRE number, you need an honest picture of your spending.

**What to include:**
- Housing (rent/mortgage, utilities, insurance)
- Food (groceries and dining out)
- Transportation (car payment, gas, insurance, or transit)
- Healthcare (premiums, out-of-pocket costs)
- Personal care, clothing, subscriptions
- Entertainment, travel, hobbies
- Taxes on investment withdrawals (often underestimated)

**What to exclude:**
- Mortgage payments if your home will be paid off by retirement
- Work-related expenses (commuting, work clothes, lunches)
- Contributions to retirement accounts (you won't be saving in retirement)

**Tip:** Look at your actual bank and credit card statements for the past 12 months. Estimates are almost always lower than reality.

## Step 2: Choose Your Withdrawal Rate

The standard 4% rate works for most cases, but your situation might call for an adjustment:

| Scenario | Suggested Rate | Multiplier |
|----------|---------------|------------|
| Standard 30-year retirement | 4% | 25× |
| Early retirement (40+ year horizon) | 3.5% | 28.5× |
| Very early retirement (50+ year horizon) | 3% | 33× |
| Conservative / uncertain about returns | 3% | 33× |

**Why lower rates for early retirees?** The Trinity Study was designed for 30-year retirements. Retiring at 40 means your portfolio might need to last 50+ years — a significantly different scenario. Many FIRE planners in their 30s and 40s target 3–3.5%.

## Real FIRE Number Examples

### Example 1: The Average American
- Annual spending: $60,000
- 4% rule: $60,000 × 25 = **$1,500,000**
- 3.5% rule (early retirement): $60,000 × 28.5 = **$1,710,000**

### Example 2: The LeanFIRE Minimalist
- Annual spending: $30,000 (optimized lifestyle, paid-off home)
- 4% rule: $30,000 × 25 = **$750,000**
- Accessible for median-income earners who start in their 20s

### Example 3: The FatFIRE Professional
- Annual spending: $120,000 (comfortable lifestyle, travel, etc.)
- 4% rule: $120,000 × 25 = **$3,000,000**
- Requires higher income or longer accumulation phase

### Example 4: The BaristaFIRE Approach
- Annual spending: $50,000
- Part-time income covering $15,000/year
- Portfolio only needs to cover $35,000
- FIRE number: $35,000 × 25 = **$875,000** — much more reachable

## Beyond the Number: Accounting for Taxes

Many FIRE calculators ignore taxes on withdrawals. Don't.

If your portfolio is mostly in pre-tax accounts (traditional 401k, IRA), you'll owe income taxes on withdrawals. A $60,000/year withdrawal might net only $50,000 after taxes.

**Rule of thumb:** Add 10–20% to your FIRE number if your savings are primarily in tax-deferred accounts. Or better yet, optimize your account mix to include Roth accounts (tax-free withdrawals) and reduce your taxable withdrawal need.

## The Faster Path: Savings Rate

Your FIRE number tells you the destination. Your savings rate tells you the speed.

| Savings Rate | Approximate Years to FIRE |
|-------------|--------------------------|
| 10% | ~45 years |
| 20% | ~37 years |
| 30% | ~28 years |
| 40% | ~22 years |
| 50% | ~17 years |
| 60% | ~12 years |
| 70% | ~8 years |

*Assumes 7% average annual return, starting from $0*

The single most powerful lever in your FIRE timeline is your savings rate — not investment returns, not stock picking. This is why FIRE practitioners focus so intensely on the gap between income and spending.
